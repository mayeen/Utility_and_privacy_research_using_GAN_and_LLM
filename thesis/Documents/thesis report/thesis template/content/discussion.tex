\paragraph{Sensitivity to Categorical Granularity.}
A practical asymmetry surfaced by the age-binning ablation
(Section~\ref{sec:results:rq2:utility:age-binning}) is that token-based LLM
synthesisers are more sensitive to upstream categorical-cardinality choices
than GAN baselines. Collapsing the ten-decade \texttt{age} encoding into
three coarse groups depressed Pythia's Gradient-Boosting TSTR AUC by
$\approx 0.065$ and macro-F1 by $\approx 0.106$, while leaving HealthGAN
and PATE-GAN approximately unchanged. The implication for healthcare data
stewards: when an LLM-based generator is in scope, preprocessing decisions
that flatten ordinal categorical variables for ``simplicity'' can silently
forfeit a substantial fraction of recoverable downstream utility, and
should therefore be validated against the dataset's native encoding before
being committed to.
