\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusion:contributions}

% Migrated and lightly edited from proposal §7 with thesis-specific scope
% (four generators rather than three).
This thesis delivers a systematic and rigorously controlled comparison of
GAN-based and LLM-based methods for privacy-preserving synthetic medical data
generation. By evaluating HealthGAN, PATE-GAN, Pythia-70M, Pythia-1B, and
Pythia-1B-DP under a unified experimental framework on the
\emph{Diabetes 130-US Hospitals} dataset~\cite{diabetes_db}, the study
establishes a transparent basis for analysing the privacy--utility trade-off
across fundamentally different generative paradigms. The integration of
aligned training conditions, consistent preprocessing, and an
eleven-metric evaluation pipeline ensures that differences in performance
reflect model characteristics rather than experimental variability.

The four concrete contributions of this work are:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0.25em
  \item \textbf{Unified evaluation pipeline.} A reproducible two-pillar
        framework --- \emph{utility} (split into statistical similarity to
        the real data and predictive performance of downstream models) and
        \emph{privacy} --- instantiated as eleven single-number metrics
        applied identically to every generator under comparison. The pipeline
        is released as the
        \href{run:../../thesis/data_analysis.ipynb}{\texttt{data\_analysis.ipynb}}
        notebook with the supporting feature-engineering module
        \href{run:../../thesis/feature_engineering.py}{\texttt{feature\_engineering.py}}.
  \item \textbf{Head-to-head benchmark.} Direct comparison of five generators
        spanning the GAN/LLM, DP/non-DP, and LLM-scale axes:
        HealthGAN~\cite{healthgan},
        PATE-GAN~\cite{pategan},
        Pythia-70M-LoRA and Pythia-1B-LoRA~\cite{Biderman2023Pythia,Hu2022LoRA,Miletic2024},
        and Pythia-1B-DP-LoRA (Opacus
        DP-SGD~\cite{Yousefpour2021Opacus,Abadi2016DPSGD}). The Pythia-1B
        non-DP scale anchor and the Pythia-1B-DP variant together
        constitute a scope extension beyond the originally submitted
        proposal, yielding the first within-family DP-cost comparison for
        tabular LLM synthesis on this benchmark at an architecturally
        controlled scale.
  \item \textbf{Within-family DP cost characterisation.} Quantitative
        evidence on the marginal cost of formal $(\varepsilon = 5.0,
        \delta = 10^{-5})$-differential privacy, separately measured for the
        GAN family (HealthGAN $\to$ PATE-GAN) and the LLM family
        (Pythia-1B $\to$ Pythia-1B-DP), with the LLM-side toggle held at
        a fixed model scale to isolate the DP-SGD effect from a
        confounding scale change.
  \item \textbf{Practical guidance.} A decision-oriented summary identifying
        when a non-DP GAN suffices, when formal DP via PATE-GAN is warranted,
        and where LLM-based synthesis remains competitive given privacy
        budgets and governance constraints.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Direct Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:conclusion:rq-answers}

% Condensed restatement of the "Answer to RQx" paragraphs from
% §\ref{sec:results:rq1:answer}, §\ref{sec:results:rq2:answer}, and
% §\ref{sec:results:rq3:answer}; the full supporting analysis stays in
% Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.
The three research questions stated in Section~\ref{sec:intro:rqs} are
answered directly below; the complete numerical evidence and figures
that support each answer are reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

\paragraph{RQ1 -- Evaluation Methods.}
The evaluation question is answered by the two-pillar framework surveyed
in Section~\ref{sec:bg:eval} and operationalised in
Section~\ref{sec:method:evaluation}: utility is assessed through
statistical similarity to the real data and predictive performance of
downstream models, while privacy is assessed separately through
instance-level distance metrics and explicit membership- and
attribute-inference simulations. Instantiated as the eleven single-number
metrics of Table~\ref{tab:rq1-coverage}, the suite is constructed so that
each metric captures a distinct data-characteristic dimension and the
branches are jointly necessary rather than individually sufficient, a
property the empirical results confirm where PATE-GAN's degenerate
distance scores coexist with its complete utility collapse. The recommended
minimal subset of these metrics that still covers both pillars without
redundancy is set out in Section~\ref{sec:results:rq1:minimal-suite}.

\paragraph{RQ2 -- Privacy--Utility Trade-Offs Across Generative Families.}
On the privacy--utility plane neither family dominates outright, but each
occupies a different region. Within the GAN family, HealthGAN is the
strongest guarantee-free operating point, attaining the best marginal
fidelity (mean KS $0.0480$), the best survival agreement (Cox
confidence-interval overlap $0.583$), respectable predictive utility (TSTR AUC-ROC
$0.631$), and the only usable distance ratio that clears the privacy
threshold (NNDR $1.066$), whereas its differentially private member
PATE-GAN collapses on both utility branches at $\varepsilon = 5.0$ and
earns only the apparent privacy of off-manifold output. Within the LLM
family, Pythia-1B lies closest to the real-data utility ceiling (AUC
$0.682$ against $0.701$) with the best joint-dependency fidelity
(correlation difference $0.0206$), Pythia-70M trails only slightly (AUC
$0.663$) despite its far smaller scale, and the differentially private
Pythia-1B-DP remains usable (AUC $0.583$) while removing its non-private
counterpart's sole attribute-inference leak. The balance is therefore a
genuine trade-off where no formal guarantee is required, but once
$(\varepsilon = 5.0, \delta = 10^{-5})$-differential privacy is demanded it
tips decisively toward the LLM family, whose private member preserves
usable utility while the GAN family's does not
(Section~\ref{sec:results:rq2:answer}).

\paragraph{RQ3 -- Within-Family Cost of Differential-Privacy Enforcement.}
At a matched budget of $(\varepsilon = 5.0, \delta = 10^{-5})$ the cost of
enforcing differential privacy is strongly family-dependent. The
LLM-family toggle (Pythia-1B $\to$ Pythia-1B-DP) is a clean test on a
shared backbone, realised at an accounted $\varepsilon = 4.998$: it costs
$0.0991$ in TSTR AUC-ROC while leaving statistical similarity essentially
intact (mean KS $+0.0187$) and survival agreement unchanged ($7/13$
confidence-interval overlaps before and after), and it converts that
moderate cost into a genuine privacy gain by removing the one
attribute-inference leak (maximum advantage $+0.0410 \to -0.0056$) and
driving outlier linkability to zero. The GAN-family toggle (HealthGAN
$\to$ PATE-GAN) instead loses $0.1204$ in AUC-ROC and collapses the rest
of the utility pillar (mean KS $+0.4901$, Cox overlap $7/12 \to 0/13$),
with no genuine privacy gain beyond the off-manifold distance artefact,
and it must be read with the caveat that it conflates the DP mechanism
with an architecture change between two distinct generators rather than
isolating differential privacy
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion:limit:gan-toggle-asymmetry}). The conditional
recommendation is therefore clear: when a formal guarantee at
$\varepsilon = 5.0$ is required on tabular medical data of this kind,
differentially private LLM fine-tuning through DP-SGD yields a generator
that remains in the usable region of the privacy--utility plane, whereas
the differentially private GAN considered here does not.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion:future}

% Concrete extensions the thesis does not pursue but flags as natural
% follow-ups, each tied to a finding or limitation of the present study.
The findings reported above suggest several concrete extensions that this
thesis does not pursue but that follow naturally from its scope and
limitations.

\subsection{Larger Language Models}
\label{sec:conclusion:future:llm}

The LLM results leave two openings that additional model scale could close.
Pythia-1B attains the highest utility of any generator yet still trails the
real-data ceiling (TSTR AUC-ROC $0.682$ against $0.701$), and DP-SGD failed
to converge usefully at the Pythia-70M scale
(Section~\ref{sec:results:rq3:pythia-70m-failure}), which forced the
differentially private comparison up to the 1B backbone. Both observations
motivate fine-tuning larger autoregressive models, such as Pythia-410M,
Pythia-1.4B, or Llama-3-8B, under the same parameter-efficient low-rank
adaptation used here. \textcite{Miletic2024} report that synthesis quality
improves with model size, and the differential-privacy literature indicates
that larger models absorb DP-SGD noise more
gracefully~\cite{Li2022LargeDP}, so a larger backbone may simultaneously
narrow the residual utility gap and reduce the differential-privacy cost
quantified in Section~\ref{sec:results:rq3:llm-toggle}. The principal
obstacle is the compute and memory overhead of per-sample-gradient DP-SGD
at that scale.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Generalisation}
\label{sec:conclusion:future:cross-data}

All results in this thesis are obtained on a single benchmark, the
Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset~\cite{diabetes_db}, so the generator
rankings reported here cannot be assumed to transfer to other clinical
settings. A natural next step is to repeat the unified pipeline on richer
electronic-health-record corpora such as MIMIC-III or MIMIC-IV~\cite{mimic}
and eICU, which contain more continuous physiological variables, higher
attribute cardinality, and explicit temporal structure. Re-running the same
two-pillar evaluation on those datasets would establish whether the LLM
family retains its utility advantage and whether the GAN-side
differential-privacy collapse observed here is dataset-specific or
systematic.

\subsection{Privacy-Budget Ablation}
\label{sec:conclusion:future:epsilon}

The privacy comparison is conducted at a single matched budget of
$(\varepsilon = 5.0, \delta = 10^{-5})$, which yields one point on each
family's privacy--utility curve rather than its slope. Repeating the two
differentially private generators, PATE-GAN and Pythia-1B-DP, across a
range of budgets such as $\varepsilon \in \{1, 3, 5, 10\}$ would trace the
trade-off explicitly, as suggested for differentially private synthesis by
\textcite{Nahid2024}. Such an ablation would reveal whether the GAN-family
collapse documented in Section~\ref{sec:results:rq3:gan-toggle} persists at
looser budgets, and would locate the budget at which the LLM family's
contained utility cost becomes prohibitive.

\subsection{Stronger Privacy Adversaries}
\label{sec:conclusion:future:adversaries}

The privacy pillar in this thesis relies on a distance-based
membership-inference adversary and a predictor-based attribute-inference
attack, both of which are comparatively weak threat models. The uniformly
negligible leakage they report (Section~\ref{sec:results:rq2:privacy})
therefore establishes a lower bound on disclosure risk rather than a
guarantee, consistent with the caution against similarity-based privacy
evaluation raised in Section~\ref{sec:bg:eval:overreliance}. Stronger
adversaries would test that bound: shadow-model membership
inference~\cite{Shokri2017MIA}, training-data reconstruction and extraction
attacks~\cite{Carlini2021Extracting}, and white-box adversaries with access
to the generator's parameters. Evaluating the same generators under these
attacks, and in particular comparing the differentially private variants
against their non-private counterparts, would show whether the formal
guarantee delivers protection that the present attacks are too weak to
detect.

\subsection{Longitudinal and Multimodal Records}
\label{sec:conclusion:future:longitudinal}

This study is restricted to a single cross-sectional tabular snapshot, in
which each patient encounter is treated as one independent row. Many
clinical questions instead depend on longitudinal trajectories and on data
beyond the tabular modality. Extending the comparison to time-series
synthesis, as explored for medical signals by \textcite{dash2020medical}
and \textcite{bhanot2022investigating}, and to multimodal records that
combine tabular fields with imaging or free-text notes, would test whether
the privacy--utility conclusions of this thesis hold once temporal and
cross-modal dependencies must also be preserved.

\subsection{Non-Linear Dependency Metrics}
\label{sec:conclusion:future:nonlinear}

The statistical-similarity branch quantifies joint structure through the linear
correlation difference. Extending it with a non-linear dependency measure, such
as a mutual-information matrix difference~\cite{kaabachi2025scoping}, would
capture attribute dependencies that linear correlation cannot, and is a natural
direction for future work.

\section{Closing Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusion:closing}

% Migrated and lightly adjusted from proposal §7 closing paragraphs.
Through a combined assessment of utility --- spanning both distributional
similarity to the real data and predictive performance of downstream
models --- alongside instance-level privacy risks, this thesis aims to
determine not only which models perform best, but under which conditions
each class of models is most appropriate. This enables a nuanced interpretation of when non-DP synthesis is
sufficient, when formal differential privacy is necessary, and how LLM-based
generators behave in terms of memorisation and disclosure risk in clinical
settings. Beyond generating comparative insights, the thesis contributes a
reusable, methodologically grounded evaluation protocol that can support
future research and practical governance. Although the study focuses on
tabular EHR-like data and a defined set of generative models, the framework
provides a foundation for subsequent extensions to longitudinal records,
multimodal data, and emerging DP-enhanced LLMs. Ultimately, the findings are
intended to inform model selection, guide responsible data-release strategies,
and strengthen the methodological basis for safe and high-utility synthetic
data use in healthcare research.
